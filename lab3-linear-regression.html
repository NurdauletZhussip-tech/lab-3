6Polynomial regression

Using your single best feature from Step 3, fit polynomial regression for degrees 1 through 5 and record train R² and test R² (5-fold CV) at each degree.

Your chosen feature's raw values span a range like 46–230. Raising that to the 5th power produces numbers around 6×10¹¹, which makes ordinary least squares numerically unstable — you'll see it as a train R² that drops with degree, which is impossible in exact arithmetic (a degree-5 polynomial can always fit at least as well as a degree-1 one on the same training data). Scale the feature first, inside a pipeline, so the instability doesn't happen: 
from sklearn.pipeline import make_pipeline
from sklearn.preprocessing import PolynomialFeatures, StandardScaler
from sklearn.linear_model import LinearRegression

pipe = make_pipeline(
    StandardScaler(),
    PolynomialFeatures(degree=degree, include_bias=False),
    LinearRegression(),
)

Plot train R² and test R² against degree on the same axes. Identify the degree where test R² stops improving, and explain in your own words why train R² keeps climbing past that point while test R² doesn't.
